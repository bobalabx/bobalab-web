01

Algorithmic Advice & Human Decisions

How should an algorithm advise people so that its advice gets used, and still leaves them better decision-makers on their own?

5 projects · 4 papers with a public version · Draft · pending lab review

Why it matters

Algorithms can often compute a better policy than the one people follow, but advice only helps if people adopt it and can still act well without it. This direction studies how to turn optimal policies into short, usable tips, how precise that advice should be, and what keeps people from following advice that is known to be good.

Questions we are asking

  • Can an algorithm read records of how people make step-by-step decisions and boil the costliest gap from the best policy down to one short tip people will use?
  • When should advice name the exact next move, and when should it give a broader rule people can carry into new situations after the advice is gone?
  • Beyond trust, what stops people from following advice that is known to be good, for example how hard the tip is to put into practice?

02

Generative & Agentic AI at Work

What does generative AI actually change in professional work, and how should authority be split between people and AI agents?

5 projects · 5 papers with a public version · Draft · pending lab review

Why it matters

Generative AI and AI agents are entering everyday professional work. This direction studies how professionals use these tools on their own tasks, where productivity gains come from, and how advice and authority should be shared between people and AI.

Questions we are asking

  • What kinds of help do teachers ask ChatGPT for on their own real tasks, and do their conversations stick to one kind?
  • Does generative AI raise productivity mainly by speeding up familiar tasks, or by making it worth switching to different tasks?
  • How should a human advisor change course when clients can also ask their own personal AI?

03

Learning on the Job with Algorithms

How do workers learn alongside algorithmic recommendations, and how can platforms assign work so that people improve?

4 projects · 4 papers with a public version · Draft · pending lab review

Why it matters

Gig workers learn on the job, often guided by an app’s recommendations. This direction studies how that learning happens in large operational datasets, and how platforms can offer and dispatch work so that workers improve over time rather than only covering today’s demand.

Questions we are asking

  • Do gig workers get faster and more reliable with experience, and do they lean on or drift away from the app's recommendations as they learn?
  • How should a platform offer jobs when workers can say no and improve at different rates the platform cannot see in advance?

04

Gig & Platform Work Design

When and why do flexible workers choose to work, and how should platforms pay, motivate and staff them?

5 projects · 5 papers with a public version · Draft · pending lab review

Why it matters

Platform workers decide when, where and for whom to work. This direction studies what drives those choices, from pay to habits and momentum, and how platforms can design pay, incentives and staffing when workers split their time across competing apps.

Questions we are asking

  • What drives a ride-hail driver's choice to log on and log off: the pay rate, an earnings target, or the momentum of a long shift?
  • When drivers split their hours across rival apps, which pay designs (hourly guarantees, streak bonuses, wage floors) change how much they drive and how often they switch?
