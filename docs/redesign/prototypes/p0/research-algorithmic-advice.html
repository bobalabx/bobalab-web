Algorithmic Advice & Human Decisions

When should advice name the exact next move, and when should it give a broader rule people can carry into new situations?

5 projects, 4 papers and 2 in progress. Shares papers with Learning on the Job (1) and AI at Work (1).

Why it matters

How algorithms should advise people through multi-step decisions so the advice gets used, helps now, and still leaves people better on their own.

A published study develops a reinforcement-learning method that reads logs of people's step-by-step choices and condenses the costliest gap from a strong policy into one short tip, then tests the tips with participants in a virtual-kitchen game. A working paper compares two ways of phrasing advice, an exact next action or a broader rule of thumb, in experiments on electric-vehicle charging routes, and asks how each shapes what people do once the advice is taken away. A second working paper looks at why people in the virtual-kitchen task pass over tips that are known to be good.

Working paper, 2026

Strategic Advice in the Age of Personal AI

Yueyang Liu and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

arXiv preprint, version 2

A game-theoretic model of a professional advisor whose client may also consult a personal AI. The advisor tilts advice to offset the AI, and is worst off when clients use the AI some of the time rather than never or always.

Working paper, 2026

Precise or Broad? The Reward-Learning Frontier in Algorithmic Advice Design

Philippe Blaettchen and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

Major revision at Manufacturing & Service Operations Management

Compares two ways of turning an optimal policy into advice in EV-charging route experiments: an exact action versus a broader rule of thumb. Exact advice performs best while it is visible; broader advice can leave people better able to act alone in similar settings once advice is removed.

Journal article, 2026

Improving Human Sequential Decision Making with Reinforcement Learning

Hamsa Bastani, Osbert Bastani and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

Management Science, 72(1), 733–755. 7 awards

An algorithm learns from logs of people's multi-step decisions and turns the biggest gap between their play and an optimal policy into one short tip. In virtual-kitchen experiments the tips raised performance, and people combined them with their own strategies.

Working paper, 2025

Understanding Non-Adoption of “Optimal” Algorithmic Advice for Problem Solving

David Lee and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

Looks at why people in the virtual-kitchen task pass over algorithm-generated tips, finding they interpret tips differently and face barriers beyond trust, such as how hard a tip is to apply and features of the task setting.
