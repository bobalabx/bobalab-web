Gig & Platform Work Design

What drives a ride-hail driver's choice to log on and log off: the pay rate, an earnings target, or the momentum of a long shift?

5 projects, 6 papers and 2 in progress. Shares papers with Learning on the Job (3).

Why it matters

When and where flexible workers choose to work, and how platforms can pay, motivate and staff them in competitive labor markets.

A published study uses ride-hailing data to model drivers' decisions to start and stop working, looking at pay alongside habits such as earnings targets and the momentum of a long shift. A working paper combines one platform's records with public New York City trip data to estimate how drivers split their time between competing apps, and uses the estimates to compare pay designs such as hourly guarantees, streak bonuses and wage floors. Park Sinchaisri's doctoral dissertation on managing the gig economy is also filed here.

Working paper, 2026

Managing Multihoming Workers in the Gig Economy

Gad Allon, Maxime C. Cohen, Ken Moon and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

SSRN working paper 4502968. Revise and resubmit at Operations Research. INFORMS TIMES Section Best Working Paper, Runner-Up, 2024

Combines one ride-hailing platform's data with public New York City trip data to estimate how drivers split time between competing apps, then tests pay designs: guaranteed hourly pay can raise supply at fixed cost, streak bonuses curb switching, and wage floors need limits on congestion.

Working paper, 2025

Learning to Gig: Workforce Development under Worker Agency

Haonan Deng, Shunan Jiang, Xinpeng Qu and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

SSRN working paper 5387320. Major revision at Manufacturing & Service Operations Management

Asks how a platform should offer jobs when workers may decline and improve at different, unknown rates. Proposes a tractable benchmark and learning algorithms, including a variant that caps the risk of market failure, and tests them on delivery-platform data.

Journal article, 2025

Learning on the Go: Understanding How Gig Economy Workers Learn with Recommendation Algorithms

Shunan Jiang and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction, 9(7), 1–35

Draws on more than a million delivery orders to show couriers get faster and more punctual with experience. Newcomers tend to follow the app's job recommendations, while seasoned workers increasingly go their own way.

Journal article, 2023

The Impact of Behavioral and Economic Drivers on Gig Economy Workers

Gad Allon, Maxime C. Cohen and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

Manufacturing & Service Operations Management, 25(4), 1376–1393. 4 awards

Uses ride-hailing data to model when on-demand drivers start and stop working. Pay matters, but so do habits such as quitting after reaching an earnings goal and staying on once a shift has momentum; incentives that account for both can add capacity without extra cost.
