Learning on the Job with Algorithms

Do gig workers get faster and more reliable with experience, and do they lean on or drift away from the app's recommendations as they learn?

4 projects, 4 papers and 1 in progress. Shares papers with Platform Work (3) and Algorithmic Advice (1).

Why it matters

How workers build skill alongside app recommendations, and how platforms can assign work so people improve instead of only covering today's demand.

A published study uses more than a million delivery orders to follow how couriers' speed and punctuality change with experience, and how their use of the app's job recommendations shifts as they gain it. A working paper studies how a platform should offer jobs when workers can turn offers down and get better at the work at different rates that the platform cannot see in advance; it proposes learning algorithms and tests them on delivery-platform data.

Working paper, 2026

Precise or Broad? The Reward-Learning Frontier in Algorithmic Advice Design

Philippe Blaettchen and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

Major revision at Manufacturing & Service Operations Management

Compares two ways of turning an optimal policy into advice in EV-charging route experiments: an exact action versus a broader rule of thumb. Exact advice performs best while it is visible; broader advice can leave people better able to act alone in similar settings once advice is removed.

Working paper, 2025

Learning to Gig: Workforce Development under Worker Agency

Haonan Deng, Shunan Jiang, Xinpeng Qu and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

SSRN working paper 5387320. Major revision at Manufacturing & Service Operations Management

Asks how a platform should offer jobs when workers may decline and improve at different, unknown rates. Proposes a tractable benchmark and learning algorithms, including a variant that caps the risk of market failure, and tests them on delivery-platform data.

Journal article, 2025

Learning on the Go: Understanding How Gig Economy Workers Learn with Recommendation Algorithms

Shunan Jiang and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction, 9(7), 1–35

Draws on more than a million delivery orders to show couriers get faster and more punctual with experience. Newcomers tend to follow the app's job recommendations, while seasoned workers increasingly go their own way.
