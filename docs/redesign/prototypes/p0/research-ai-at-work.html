Generative & Agentic AI at Work

What kinds of help do teachers ask ChatGPT for on their own real tasks, and do their conversations stick to one kind?

5 projects, 4 papers and 3 in progress. Shares papers with Algorithmic Advice (1).

Why it matters

How professionals actually use generative AI and AI assistants, what it changes about their work, and how much authority AI agents should hold.

A published study follows K-12 teachers as they use ChatGPT on their own tasks and sorts the kinds of help they ask for. A working paper treats lesson preparation as a choice among tasks and draws on a year-long case study of teachers to examine how generative AI changes their productivity. A game-theoretic preprint models a professional advisor whose clients can also consult a personal AI.

Working paper, 2026

Strategic Advice in the Age of Personal AI

Yueyang Liu and Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri

arXiv preprint, version 2

A game-theoretic model of a professional advisor whose client may also consult a personal AI. The advisor tilts advice to offset the AI, and is worst off when clients use the AI some of the time rather than never or always.

Working paper, 2026

The Teacher's Knapsack: Generative AI and Teacher Productivity

Samantha Keppler, Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri and Clare Snyder

SSRN working paper 4924786

Frames lesson preparation as picking the quickest set of tasks that meets a learning goal. A year-long case study of 24 teachers suggests generative AI helps both by speeding familiar tasks and by making it worthwhile to switch to new ones, though switching costs hold some teachers back.

Journal article, 2025

Making ChatGPT Work for Me

Samantha Keppler, Wichinpong Park Sinchaisri and Clare Snyder

Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction, 9(2), 1–23

Observes 24 K-12 teachers using ChatGPT on their own work and sorts their requests into a handful of distinct ways of asking for help; most conversations stay within one of them.
