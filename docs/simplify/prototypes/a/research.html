Research

Four lines of research, one question running through all of them: how do people learn to work with algorithms and AI? We answer it with field data from gig platforms and controlled experiments, drawing on operations management, behavioral science, economics, computer science and human–computer interaction.

  1. Algorithmic Advice & Human Decisions

    When should advice name the exact next move, and when should it give a broader rule people can carry into new situations?

    How algorithms should advise people through multi-step decisions so the advice gets used, helps now, and still leaves people better on their own.

    4 papers, 2 in progress
    5 projects

  2. Generative & Agentic AI at Work

    What kinds of help do teachers ask ChatGPT for on their own real tasks, and do their conversations stick to one kind?

    How professionals actually use generative AI and AI assistants, what it changes about their work, and how much authority AI agents should hold.

    5 papers, 2 in progress
    5 projects

  3. Learning on the Job with Algorithms

    Do gig workers get faster and more reliable with experience, and do they lean on or drift away from the app’s recommendations as they learn?

    How workers build skill alongside app recommendations, and how platforms can assign work so people improve instead of only covering today’s demand.

    4 papers, 1 in progress
    4 projects

  4. Gig & Platform Work Design

    What drives a ride-hail driver’s choice to log on and log off: the pay rate, an earnings target, or the momentum of a long shift?

    When and where flexible workers choose to work, and how platforms can pay, motivate and staff them in competitive labor markets.

    6 papers, 2 in progress
    5 projects
