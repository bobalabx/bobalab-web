Research

Four questions, one thread: how people learn to work with algorithms. Each direction below links to its own page with every paper, project and person. If you like seeing the whole picture at once, the timeline at the end shows how the four lines grew and where they meet.

Directions

All 18 projects

  1. Algorithmic Advice & Human Decisions

    4 papers, 2 in progress

    When should an algorithm give exact advice, and when a rule of thumb?

    How algorithms should advise people through multi-step decisions so the advice gets used, helps now, and still leaves people better on their own.

    For example: When should advice name the exact next move, and when should it give a broader rule people can carry into new situations?

    • Precise or Broad? The Reward-Learning Frontier in Algorithmic Advice Design
    • Strategic Advice in the Age of Personal AI
    • Improving Human Sequential Decision Making with Reinforcement Learning

  2. Generative & Agentic AI at Work

    5 papers, 2 in progress

    How do people really use AI at work, and how much should it decide on its own?

    How professionals actually use generative AI and AI assistants, what it changes about their work, and how much authority AI agents should hold.

    For example: What kinds of help do teachers ask ChatGPT for on their own real tasks, and do their conversations stick to one kind?

    • Stale, Misattributed, or Late: Where Personal Memory Fails Before Generation
    • The Teacher’s Knapsack: Generative AI and Teacher Productivity
    • Making ChatGPT Work for Me

  3. Learning on the Job with Algorithms

    4 papers, 1 in progress

    Do workers learn from the app, or learn to outgrow it?

    How workers build skill alongside app recommendations, and how platforms can assign work so people improve instead of only covering today’s demand.

    For example: Do gig workers get faster and more reliable with experience, and do they lean on or drift away from the app’s recommendations as they learn?

    • Dispatching to Develop: Worker Agency and Workforce Development in Gig Platforms
    • Learning on the Go: Understanding How Gig Economy Workers Learn with Recommendation Algorithms
    • Learning to Gig: Workforce Development under Worker Agency

  4. Gig & Platform Work Design

    6 papers, 2 in progress

    What gets gig workers to log on, and what keeps them on?

    When and where flexible workers choose to work, and how platforms can pay, motivate and staff them in competitive labor markets.

    For example: What drives a ride-hail driver’s choice to log on and log off: the pay rate, an earnings target, or the momentum of a long shift?

    • Managing Multihoming Workers in the Gig Economy
    • The Impact of Behavioral and Economic Drivers on Gig Economy Workers
    • Managing the Gig Economy via Behavioral and Operational Lenses
